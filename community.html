Community Guidelines

Last updated: September 13, 2026

FindHue is a small, friendly game about noticing color in the everyday world around you. These guidelines exist so everyone's daily photo feels safe to open. They apply to every Find you publish, every report you file, and every interaction with friends and Friend Groups.

What you can publish

Publish a genuine photo taken for today's FindHue challenge. The whole image does not need to consist exclusively of the target color. Be thoughtful about photographing other people: obtain permission before making someone the clear subject, and never photograph someone where they reasonably expect privacy.

What's never okay

  • Sexual or exploitative content, child sexual abuse material (CSAM), or the sexualization, exploitation, or grooming of minors. Normal, non-exploitative family photos are not prohibited merely because a child appears.
  • Graphic violence or gore, cruelty, or glorification of serious harm.
  • Encouragement, instruction, or glorification of self-harm or suicide.
  • Harassment or bullying of anyone, including other FindHue users.
  • Hateful content that attacks people based on race, ethnicity, religion, gender, sexual orientation, disability, or similar characteristics.
  • Threats of violence or harm against any person or group.
  • Illegal activity, or content depicting or promoting it.
  • Impersonation of another person, or misrepresenting who you are.
  • Spam — repeated, irrelevant, or promotional content that isn't a genuine attempt at the daily challenge.
  • Violations of someone else's privacy or intellectual property — publishing a photo you don't have the right to share, or that identifies someone without their consent.
  • Attempting to bypass or trick our automated moderation — for example, publishing an image crafted specifically to slip past screening.
  • Malicious, false, or abusive reports, or misuse of safety tools.

How this is enforced

  • Every Find is screened before friends can see it. A genuine moderation rejection prevents publication and remains in your private local history. A transient moderation or network failure is held back rather than approved; FindHue may retry it when the app returns to the foreground. Passing automated moderation is not an endorsement, and automated systems can make mistakes.
  • You can report any Find you see from a friend, right from the app — tell us why, and add a note if it helps. Reports go to us for review; the person you report is never told who reported them.
  • You can block anyone, at any time, from Settings or directly from their profile. Blocking restricts relevant FindHue activity in both directions. Blocked users can be managed from Settings, but blocking does not necessarily remove either person from a shared Friend Group.
  • We review reported content and accounts and may refuse publication, remove content, warn, restrict, suspend, or terminate an account proportionately. Serious cases, including child sexual exploitation or abuse, credible violence threats, severe harassment, serious unlawful activity, deliberate moderation bypass, or significant safety-system abuse may receive immediate action. Unlawful material or activity may be reported to authorities where required by law.

We aim to review reports promptly, prioritizing urgent safety, exploitation, and threat reports, but review time may vary. For immediate danger, contact appropriate local emergency services and then use in-app reporting or Support.

Appeals

Contact Support to request review of a suspension, restriction, or termination. An appeal may provide context for reconsideration, but not every appeal will result in reversal.

The spirit of it

FindHue works because it's a low-stakes, genuinely fun way to notice the world with friends. If you're ever unsure whether something's okay to publish, a good rule of thumb: would you be comfortable if the person in the photo, or the friend seeing it, knew exactly how and why you took it? If not, don't publish it.

Questions

If you're unsure whether something's allowed, or want to appeal an account action, contact us — see Support.
